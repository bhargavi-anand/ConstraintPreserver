Interim Guidance on the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

There is significant promise in the use of artificial intelligence, and as with all tools, responsible, equitable, and ethical use is essential to the university’s role and mission. As the University works to finalize its guidance, these interim guidelines provide students, faculty, and staff with a framework for responsible use of AI tools. This guidance was developed with the support of the Responsible Use of AI Working Group, which was formed by the Chief Information Officer and Acting Provost to advise and make recommendations on responsible use of AI to the President’s Cabinet.

Use of AI tools to support work at American University should be conducted ethically, in compliance with all university policies, and with transparency and accountability.

No Decisions Without Humans

Outcomes generated by artificial intelligence must be subject to human oversight. No decisions impacting real persons at American University, including employment, admissions, or students, may be made without human oversight and accountability.

Data Usage

Information provided to an AI tool should be treated as public.

Confidential or Official Use data must not be used in an AI platform unless approved by the University. Many AI tools do not provide sufficient security, and data may be shared outside of the university in unexpected ways.

When uncertainty exists, data should not be entered into or shared with an AI platform until discussion has occurred with a supervisor or department or division leadership.

Academic Integrity

Student use of generative artificial intelligence is not prohibited by the Academic Integrity Code, and there is no single university-wide policy governing academic use of AI. Faculty are responsible for communicating course policies related to generative AI and for providing clear syllabus and assignment guidance regarding appropriate use.

The Academic Integrity Code requires that students submit work that authentically represents their capabilities in response to assignments. Student use of generative AI without permission, or use of AI to replace work assumed to be their own, may constitute a violation of the Academic Integrity Code.

When faculty assign the use of AI tools for coursework, such use should be structured to prioritize equity. Tools that require payment should be covered by the department.

Employee Use

Employees must be transparent in their use of artificial intelligence. Work products created wholly or partially using AI tools must be disclosed to unit leadership, including disclosure of how AI was used when appropriate.

Users are responsible for validating the accuracy of AI-generated content using trusted first-party sources and for monitoring the reliability of that content. Users remain accountable for the use of AI-generated content and for the impact of that use on their work.

Privacy

Many AI tools and services use user input and data to train their models. Data entered into AI tools, including personal data, may not be retrievable or deletable. Some tools may also access data from other open windows or applications on a user’s device.
