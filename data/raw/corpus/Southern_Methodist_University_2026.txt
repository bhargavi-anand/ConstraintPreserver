Purpose

This guidance document outlines the ethical, responsible, and secure use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) within the University. This guidance aims to support the University’s mission of education, research, and community service while protecting all stakeholders' integrity, privacy, and rights. It covers the use of AI tools, including but not limited to machine learning models, natural language processing systems, and AI-based search and administrative tools.

Recommended Do and Don’t

The following shares a list of dos and don’ts when considering generative AI tools and subscriptions for university business.

DOs

DO redact or anonymize personally identifiable information (student IDs, Social Security numbers, health information) before pasting or uploading anything into any AI tool.

DO use SMU-approved AI tools for work, including Microsoft Copilot and SMU GPT business tenants, and never use personal free accounts, to ensure institutional protections apply.

DO use institution-approved notetakers, such as Copilot or Zoom, with participant consent and review notes before sharing.

DO check all AI output for accuracy and verify facts before sharing AI-generated results with colleagues or students.

DO use AI to streamline low-risk administrative tasks, such as summarizing public policies, generating ideas, or drafting emails that do not include sensitive data.

DO report suspected misuse, including accidental exposure of personally identifiable information to AI tools, to OIT Security immediately.

DO contact OIT for support.

DON’Ts

DON’T enter sensitive, protected, regulated, confidential, or proprietary data into AI tools.

DON’T use, purchase, or subscribe to AI tools that have not been reviewed and approved by OIT.

DON’T upload student records, grades, or advising notes, as all are protected by FERPA.

DON’T paste confidential human resources or payroll data into AI prompts.

DON’T assume AI outputs are private. Even in Microsoft Copilot or SMU GPT accounts, outputs may be logged or visible to administrators.

DON’T rely on AI to make final legal, financial, or compliance decisions for the University or for personal matters.

DON’T use personal accounts, such as free consumer AI tools, for any work involving institutional data.

DON’T assume that information available online or in public sources is free to use without considering copyright, licensing, or attribution requirements.

DON’T enter SMU usernames, passwords, or multi-factor authentication codes into AI agents, chatbots, or third-party applications that request credentials.

DON’T use unapproved AI notetakers or rely on AI-generated notes without human review.

Key Considerations

Privacy Violations

Artificial intelligence models can inadvertently reveal sensitive information when trained on personal data, leading to privacy breaches. Synthetic data generated by AI can sometimes be reverse-engineered to identify individuals, underscoring the need for caution and awareness.

Intellectual Property and Copyright Issues

Generative AI can produce content that closely resembles or directly copies existing works, potentially violating intellectual property rights. This creates legal risks and challenges related to ownership of AI-generated content.

Privacy Concerns

Generative AI can create realistic synthetic data that includes personal information, raising significant privacy concerns. If mishandled, such data may be misused for identity theft, surveillance, or other harmful activities.

Security Risk

AI tools can be used to create or amplify cyber threats, including phishing scams, social engineering attacks, deepfakes, and malicious code. These threats are often highly convincing and difficult to detect. Users should exercise caution when AI systems request access permissions, links, or credentials and should never share authentication information with AI platforms.

Bias

AI models may inherit biases present in their training data, leading to unfair or discriminatory outcomes. Outputs should be reviewed critically, particularly when used for academic, administrative, or decision-making purposes, to avoid reinforcing bias.

Accuracy

Users must validate the accuracy of AI-generated content using trusted first-party sources. Users are accountable for all content, code, images, and media produced by AI tools and should remain alert to hallucinations, misinformation, or fabricated citations.

Code Development

Generative AI may assist with writing software code. However, AI-generated code may contain errors, security vulnerabilities, or design flaws. All code must be reviewed carefully, ideally by multiple reviewers, before use.

Institutional Data

Institutional data use is governed by acceptable use and data governance policies. These policies prohibit uploading institutional data into non-SMU-sanctioned AI products. Users must exercise care in determining what data is shared with AI tools.

Business Process

AI may support business process improvement and automation. Tools, processes, and outputs must be evaluated for reliability, accuracy, consistency, privacy, and alignment with institutional requirements.

Ethics and Responsible Use

AI use at the University must align with institutional values of fairness, integrity, and transparency. Users should continually evaluate whether AI use cases align with the University’s mission and ethical expectations.

Transparency and Disclosure

When AI tools contribute to writing, analysis, or creative work, their use must be disclosed. Transparency supports academic and professional integrity and builds trust.

Caution: AI Hallucination in Generative AI Usage

AI hallucinations refer to instances where generative AI produces false or misleading information, even when models are well trained.

Verification Protocol

AI-generated content must be cross-verified with trusted sources before use.

Awareness and Training

Users should be educated to recognize indicators of AI hallucinations and to question implausible outputs.

Limit Use for Critical Tasks

AI should not be relied upon as the sole source for decision-making in high-stakes or high-risk scenarios.

Iterative Review

A multi-stage review process involving both AI outputs and human oversight should be used to ensure accuracy and reliability.

This caution is integral to maintaining the integrity and reliability of academic work and administrative processes where AI tools are used.
