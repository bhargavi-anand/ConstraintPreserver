Rowan University recently adopted a new Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy that establishes rules around how institutional data may be used in tools like ChatGPT.
The policy, which was jointly issued by the Division of Information Resources & Technology and the Office of the Provost, states:
1	Only Rowan data classified as public may be used in non-approved AI tools. 
2	Data of any classification type other than public may only be used in approved AI tools.


Appropriate and acceptable use of G-AI tools includes the following:
•	Research or learn something new, and/or help your own learning and understanding of any topic.
•	Idea brainstorming for projects, proposals, discussion points, etc.
•	Literature searches to suggest papers or summarize academic content; however, literature review and final citations must always be your synthesis of the reviewed content, and be confirmed from verifiable and reputable sources (e.g., Google Scholar, IEEE, arXiv).
•	For starting or creating a general/routine structure for coding, and/or debugging your code, so long as the substantial/vast majority (75% or higher, not counting the documentation part) of the code is yours, and that *you* understand, test, and document the code entirely and thoroughly. You will be asked to explain your code and demonstrate your understanding, and thereby prove that it is your own work.
•	Routine proof-reading for correcting grammar, typos, clarifying or correcting sentences, so long as the original sentences are yours.
•	Generating non-technical figures to be used as concept illustrations (subject to course-specific permissions - check with the course instructor)
•	Any assignment or exercise where the instructor specifically and explicitly asks you to use an AI tool for a specific task or purpose that is aligned with course objectives and/or outcomes.
Use of G-AI tools for any of the following is inappropriate and not allowed:
The following is a set of examples and not an exhaustive list. Some overlap and repetition among the following items is deliberate and intended for reinforcement. If you are unsure whether a particular G-AI use case is appropriate, please reach out to your course professor.
•	Any use of a G-AI tool that is not properly and thoroughly cited (see below for guidelines on how to cite AI-generated content).
•	Solving or answering any questions in any graded assignment, quiz, exam, or report.
•	Analyzing data, drawing conclusions based on experiments or data analysis to be used in any graded assignment.
•	Answering any essay-type questions (other than for language clarification as indicated above) in any graded assignment, quiz, exam, or report.
•	Determining impact factors, ethical and professional considerations for any design-related course (including Embedded Systems, Engineering Clinics, Professionalism & Consulting, Frontiers in Engineering) that asks you to articulate your design decisions, professional judgment, and ethical discussions in consideration of impact factors. 
•	Code or code fragments that exceed 25% of the total script for any programming assignment or any project that requires you to develop code. If the instructor provides you with a base code to modify or to add to, then the modifications or additions cannot be AI-generated and must all be your work.
•	Uploading class/course material, lecture notes to any unauthorized G-AI tool (e.g., ChatGPT and others), or online learning platform (including Chegg, CourseHero, and other similar platforms).
•	Mathematical derivations submitted as part of a graded assignment, quiz, exam, or report.
•	Algorithm design submitted as part of a graded assignment, quiz, exam, or report.
•	Using AI to generate any part or section of a lab report, project report, or any other submission. For example, uploading raw data, results, or incomplete notes into AI to generate a report is not permitted.
•	Using AI to interpret, process, or analyze results from simulations.
It is worth noting that:
1	Pretending or presenting any content as if it is yours that is, in fact, generated by others (including Gen AI) is plagiarism. Paraphrasing or rewording the borrowed content to hide its original source is not only plagiarism, but also academic dishonesty. Either case is a form of academic integrity violation that will not be tolerated and will be reported to the Provost’s Office in accordance with Rowan University's Academic Integrity Policy.
2	Lecture notes made available to you are the intellectual property of the course developer/instructor. These lecture notes and materials are copyrighted and protected by copyright law, regardless of whether a copyright notice is visible (as is the case for any work of authorship under US law). When provided to you (such as on Canvas), you are merely given the very limited right to use them for your own personal learning. Therefore, uploading any course content to any online portal, including online learning platforms such as Course Hero, Chegg, etc., or any Gen AI platforms (such as ChatGPT, CoPilot, Gemini, etc.) is illegal and strictly prohibited. If detected, it will be reported as an Academic Integrity Violation at Level 3 or Level 4. The instructor (or content owner) may also independently take legal action against you under Copyright Law.  Simply stated: do not upload any course materials/content to any unauthorized site without the express written permission of the instructor/copyright holder.
Citing G-AI tools:
In all cases, the specific use of a G-AI tool must be properly and completely cited as follows:
•	Citing routine use of a built-in G-AI tool to correct individual errors for grammar, typos, clarification, etc., use the following format with only one citation for the entire document.
<Company name)>, <Name of the tool>. Accessed on <date in which you          used the tool>, Model or Version: <model or version used>, <descriptor>. Available at:        <link to the main page for the tool>. Context: provide a brief description of the use.
•	Example 1: Grammarly Inc, Grammarly. Accessed on 15 June 2025, Version: 14.1232.0 (browser), large language model-based writing assistant integrated into Word. Available at:  https://www.grammarly.com. Context: used for routine grammar /typo correction.
◦	Example 2: Google, Gemini. Accessed on 20 July 2025, Model: Gemini 2.0 Flash, large language model-based writing assistant integrated into Google Docs. Available at: https://gemini.google.com/app. Context: used for routine correction for grammar and typos, and obtaining a more concise writing.
•	For all other and more substantial uses of G-AI tools, use the following format, and include a separate citation for each use. If you are using up to three prompts, include all. For longer conversations, include the first, second, and last prompt with ellipses (...) in between.  For additional clarification, when needed, provide context. If asked by the faculty, include the entire transcript as an appendix.
<Company name as author)>, <Name of the tool>, “<exact prompt used as title.>” Accessed on <date in which you used the tool>, Model or Version <model or version used>, <descriptor>. Available at: <link to the main page for the tool>.  (if applicable, Context: provide a brief explanation), (If applicable, add: complete transcript provided in Appendix).
•	Example 1: OpenAI, ChatGPT, “Can you explain the practical meaning of eigenvectors and eigenvalues with examples at a level appropriate for someone who is in the second year of an engineering program?,” Accessed on 1 July 2025. Model GPT-4o, large language model-based AI chatbot. Available at: https://chatgpt.com/
◦	Example 2: Microsoft, CoPilot, “Help me understand Maxwell’s equations”, “Can you explain each equation with more context?”, …, “Which of these equations directly apply to an induction stove, and what is the principle of operation?” Accessed on 10 June 2025. Version Copilot Pro, large language model-based AI chatbot. Available at: https://copilot.microsoft.com/
◦	Example 3: Google, Gemini, “When plotting my FFT in Matlab, the horizontal axis simply shows the index. How do I get it to show the actual frequency?” Accessed on 17 June 2025, Model Gemini 2.5 Pro (preview), large language model-based AI chatbot. Available at: https://gemini.google.com/app.  Context: partial code-fragment provided by this tool is used in FIR filter design, complete transcript is available in Appendix A, the lines obtained from this tool are indicated in the code.
◦	Example 4: OpenAI, ChatGPT, “Can you provide base code in C++ for the Arduino IDE to run on an ESP32 Nano that monitors two digital inputs and sets two digital outputs based on their states? I’m a second-year ECE student learning embedded programming,” Accessed on 13 June 2025. Model GPT-4o, large language model-based AI chatbot. Available at: https://chatgpt.com/. Context: partial code-fragment is used in creating a base code for Arduino, complete transcript is available in Appendix B, and the lines obtained from this tool are indicated in the code.
