Guidelines for Safe and Responsible Usage of Generative AI at Marquette University
For Faculty, Staff, and Students

Introduction

Given the recent advancements in artificial intelligence, Marquette University realizes it is crucial to establish guidelines that ensure the safe and responsible usage of Generative AI (GenAI) tools. These guidelines apply to all faculty, staff, and students and aim to protect university data, ensure content accuracy and ownership, maintain academic integrity, and provide protocols for procuring and acquiring AI tools.

Protection of University Data

Data Sensitivity and Confidentiality

GenAI tools must be used in a manner that protects the sensitivity and confidentiality of university data. Users must ensure that data fed into AI systems does not include personally identifiable information (PII), electronic personal health information (ePHI), confidential student records, or proprietary university information.

Data Security Practices

Faculty, staff, and students must adhere to university policies and procedures when using generative AI tools. This includes using secure platforms to prevent unauthorized disclosure of university data to GenAI systems. IT Services will use available tools to monitor the usage of GenAI tools on university-owned equipment to ensure that confidential or controlled information is not uploaded into GenAI systems that have not been properly vetted and reviewed.

Compliance with University Policies and Procedures

Generative AI should be used in a manner that aligns with Marquette University’s mission, values, and Jesuit identity and must adhere to existing university policies. These include policies related to academic integrity, protection of confidential information, and acceptable use. When using GenAI tools, any input or activity must adhere to the terms of service and use policies established by the tool providers. These terms outline acceptable use and restrictions associated with each tool. IT Services serves as a resource for reviewing these terms.

Responsibility for Content Accuracy and Ownership

Verification of AI-Generated Content

Users are responsible for verifying the accuracy of content generated by AI tools. Generative AI can produce plausible yet incorrect information. Users must cross-reference AI outputs with reliable sources and ensure factual accuracy before dissemination.

Intellectual Property and Copyright

Content created using generative AI may involve complex ownership considerations. Users must be aware of intellectual property rights associated with AI-generated content and ensure compliance with copyright laws. Infringement, whether intentional or not, may result in legal claims against users or the university. When uncertainty exists, users should consult the university’s legal counsel.

Academic Integrity and Ethical Considerations

CTL and the Academic Integrity Office

The Academic Integrity Office and the Center for Teaching and Learning have partnered to create a guiding statement and instructor resources to promote shared understanding of acceptable and unacceptable use of large language model–based chatbots in coursework at Marquette University.

Bias and Fairness

Generative AI may produce biased or discriminatory content. Bias may result from training data or reflect harmful social inequities. Users must ensure that AI-generated materials adhere to principles of fairness, diversity, and inclusion and remain attentive to implicit and explicit bias in outputs.

Misinformation

Generative AI should not be used as an independent or primary source. AI systems may produce hallucinations in which outputs appear logical and coherent but are not based on factual information. Users must verify the accuracy and authenticity of AI-generated content before incorporating it into academic or administrative materials.

Sensitive Topics

Caution must be exercised when using generative AI to explore sensitive topics. Generated content must be respectful and must not cause harm or offense. Any decision resulting in an outcome for a person, including but not limited to grades, employment, discipline, or promotions, must be made by a human and not by an AI system.

Citation and Attribution

Students and faculty who choose to use generative AI tools for course assignments, academic work, or other forms of published writing must give careful attention to how AI-generated output is acknowledged and cited. Students must consult with instructors prior to using GenAI tools in coursework.

While norms and conventions for citing AI-generated content are still developing, major style guides have released preliminary guidance. Individual publishers may also provide specific citation requirements.

Procuring and Acquiring Generative AI Tools

Approval and Vetting Process

The procurement of generative AI tools must undergo an approval and vetting process to ensure compliance with university security standards. Generative AI tools, including embedded AI, must be reviewed by IT Services, the Office of the General Counsel, and Purchasing to ensure contractual guarantees are met. These guarantees include retention of data ownership, secure data storage and processing, on-demand data deletion, and prevention of university data use for large language model training.

Conclusion

Responsible use of generative AI at Marquette University requires collective effort to protect data, ensure content accuracy and ownership, uphold academic integrity, and follow proper procurement processes. Adherence to these guidelines enables the university community to leverage AI technologies while maintaining ethical and responsible practices.
