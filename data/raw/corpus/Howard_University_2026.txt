Initial Howard University Guidelines for Use of Generative AI Tools

Overview

Generative artificial intelligence tools are increasingly available and may be used in academic and administrative contexts. These tools can produce content that appears credible but may be inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading. Members of the Howard University community are expected to use generative AI responsibly and in alignment with university policies and academic standards.

Academic Integrity

The use of generative AI raises academic integrity concerns when work submitted for academic credit is presented as an individual’s own work but was substantially generated by artificial intelligence without authorization. Such use may constitute academic dishonesty under the Howard University Academic Code of Student Conduct.

Students are expected to follow instructor guidance regarding the use of generative AI in coursework. Faculty may inquire into the authorship of student work where AI use is suspected. Transparency, fairness, and adherence to established academic integrity procedures are required when addressing potential violations.

Use of generative AI does not replace the need for critical thinking, original analysis, or proper citation of sources. AI-generated content should not be relied upon as authoritative without independent verification.

Security and Phishing Risks

Generative AI tools increase the sophistication and volume of phishing and social engineering attacks. Members of the university community should remain vigilant, follow institutional cybersecurity guidance, and report suspicious communications through appropriate university channels.

Conclusion

Generative AI tools may support academic and administrative activities when used responsibly. All users are expected to exercise judgment, maintain academic integrity, and comply with university policies.
